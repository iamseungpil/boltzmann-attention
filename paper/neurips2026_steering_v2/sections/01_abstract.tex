\begin{abstract}
Multi-tool agent prompts require the model to emit more than one tool in sequence, so the central problem is coverage rather than single-step ranking. We evaluate an ontology-guided query-space bias that suppresses ontology-aligned query components at \texttt{q\_proj} and compare it against a stationary key-side bias on the same ontology basis. On MetaTool Subtask4 with 497 two-tool queries, the query-side bias improves macro F1 from 0.7307 to 0.7471 on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and from 0.6227 to 0.6271 on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, whereas the stationary key-side bias is negative on both models, dropping to 0.6850 on Qwen and 0.3105 on Llama. The result is basis-specific rather than a generic low-rank effect: on Qwen, replacing the ontology basis with a feature-shuffled or random control reduces the query-side result to 0.7254 and 0.7068, while the corresponding key-side controls collapse to 0.0000. Additional diagnostics show that the useful region is narrow, with the best Q-only point at $\beta=-0.1$, and that the real ontology perturbation is larger than the null controls in effective attention magnitude, ruling out the explanation that it succeeds merely because it perturbs less. The paper's claim is therefore narrow but coherent: for multi-tool selection, query-side coverage bias is the productive direction, stationary key-side amplification is not, and the ontology basis matters because it identifies a specific stable subspace rather than an arbitrary projection family.
\end{abstract}
