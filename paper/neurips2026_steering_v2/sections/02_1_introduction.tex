\section{1. Introduction}
Enterprise AI agents select \textbf{one or more tools} per query. The MetaTool benchmark explicitly separates these regimes: Subtask1 asks for a single tool, Subtask4 asks for two tools in sequence. In deployed settings (Netsru Gemma-3-27B agent, Appendix E), multi-tool queries dominate: a query about Tesla and stock impact needs \texttt{NewsTool} \textbf{and} \texttt{FinanceTool}; a query about news background music needs \texttt{NewsTool} \textbf{and} \texttt{MusicTool}. The paper's primary contribution addresses this multi-selection regime directly.


\subsection{1.0 Why SEKA-class K-side spectral steering cannot multi-select}

Every K-side spectral steering method in the literature — \textbf{SEKA} (Li 2026 ICLR; $k' = k + gPk$ via contrastive SVD projection), \textbf{AdaSEKA} (Kim 2026; query-adaptive expert mixture), \textbf{Focus Directions} (Zhu 2025; additive K and Q bias at top-k heads), and our own K-bias operator — is a \textbf{stationary operator}: it applies the same perturbation at every decoding step. Under autoregressive generation with KV caching, a stationary K-bias that boosts attention toward facet $A$-aligned keys at step 1 **continues to boost attention toward facet $A$** at steps 2, 3, …. If the model emits \texttt{NewsTool} at step 1 (facet $A = \text{information-delivery}$), at step 2 it will attend to the same facet $A$-aligned keys and emit a second \texttt{NewsTool}-family tool rather than a facet-$B$ tool for \texttt{MusicTool}. This is precisely the empirical pattern we observe: our K-bias at $\alpha_K=0.3$ produces $\Delta F_1 = -4.6$pp on Qwen Subtask4 multi-tool and $\Delta F_1 = -31.2$pp catastrophic on Llama Subtask4, while SEKA at amp=1.0 produces $\Delta F_1 = -7.95$pp on Llama Subtask4 partial. K-side stationary steering is \emph{structurally incapable} of facet coverage.


\subsection{1.1 Step-adaptive Q-coverage as the multi-selection axis}

We introduce a \textbf{step-adaptive Q-side operator} — the first in the steering literature — which at decoding step $t$ subtracts from the query $q_t$ the projection onto facets of \emph{already-emitted} tools $\{f_s\}_{s < t}$: $$\Delta_Q^{(t)} := -\beta \sum_{s<t} P_{f_s} q_t, \qquad P_{f_s} := B_{f_s} B_{f_s}^\top,$$ where $B_{f_s}$ is the per-head ontology column block for facet $f_s$ extracted from the \textbf{ontology basis} $B_\mathrm{ont}$. This operator structurally supports multi-selection: after emitting \texttt{NewsTool} at step 1, the step-2 query is modified to reduce its overlap with the information-delivery facet, redirecting attention to the remaining facets and enabling \texttt{MusicTool} emission. Under Hypothesis (R) (Lipschitz soft-gate) and (H-cat) (bimodal facet-channel distribution), we prove Q-coverage is the first-order optimal single-channel operator for multi-tool emission (Theorem 6.17).


\textbf{Empirical verification}. On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct Subtask4 (N=497), Q-coverage at $\beta = -0.1$ achieves F1 = 0.747 (baseline 0.731, $+$\textbf{1.64pp}) with three-tier null-control direction-specificity gap $+$4.0pp (real $\beta=-0.1$ vs random $\beta=-0.1$ vs featshuffle $\beta=-0.1$). The same operator on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct Subtask4 achieves $+$\textbf{0.40pp} (cross-family signal, smaller magnitude consistent with Llama's more concentrated attention regime; §5.5.3). In contrast, SEKA amp=1.0 on Llama Subtask4 partial gives $-$\textbf{7.95pp} — a gap of $+$8.35pp in favor of step-adaptive Q-coverage, arising from the structural difference \emph{not} from hyperparameter tuning. A third Q-side mechanism (soft M-of-2 Q-side routing at $T=0.1$) achieves $+$**3.7pp F1 / $+$4.8pp Exact** on Qwen Subtask4, corroborating the broader Q-side viability but as a more engineered variant.


\subsection{1.2 Ontology basis: tri-role orthogonality}

The same per-head ontology basis $B_\mathrm{ont}$ (rank $R \approx 24$, built once from facet annotations via Gram-Schmidt, §4.1) parameterizes three orthogonal roles:


\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Q-steering (multi-tool)}: $\Delta_Q^{(t)}$ above. Primary contribution. \textbf{Accuracy lift +1.64pp Qwen / +0.40pp Llama on Subtask4 N=497}.
\item \textbf{K-stability diagnostic}: at $\alpha=0.3$, $\mathrm{span}(B_\mathrm{ont})$ is the unique rank-$R$ K-perturbation subspace preserving FC-emission (F1 0.685); random and feature-shuffled controls of equal magnitude collapse to F1 0.000 (direction-specificity gap \textbf{+68.5pp} on Subtask4 N=497). This is a stability property (Cor 6.9.6), not an accuracy lift. It establishes $B_\mathrm{ont}$ as a \emph{uniquely privileged subspace} geometrically.
\item \textbf{K-compression axis}: OCQ (1-bit categorical on $B_\mathrm{ont}$ + KIVI-style residual) achieves $-4.37$ PPL over KIVI at 2-bit (Qwen2.5-7B WT2, Thm 6.13). Attention-weighted bit allocation (Thm 6.18) extends this.
\end{itemize}

Theorem 6.19 establishes \textbf{single-basis sufficiency}: $B_\mathrm{ont}$ — constructed \emph{once} — realizes all three roles with zero asymptotic overhead.


\subsection{1.3 Contributions (6, focused)}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Main Step-adaptive Q-coverage for multi-tool selection (Thm 6.17, §3.6.1, main novelty)}. The operator $\Delta_Q^{(t)} = -\beta \sum_{s<t} P_{f_s} q_t$ is first-order optimal for multi-tool log-likelihood under (R) and (H-cat). \emph{Verified at full 497}: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct Subtask4 $\beta = -0.1$ → F1 = 0.747 ($+$1.64pp over no\_steer 0.731), null-control gap $+$4.0pp. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct Subtask4 $\beta = -0.1$ → F1 $+$0.40pp. No prior art on step-adaptive Q-side coverage: Focus Directions (Zhu 2025) is stationary; SEKA-family is K-side. On the same benchmark, stationary K-side methods (our K-bias, SEKA) are negative ($-$4.6 to $-$31.2pp) by autoregressive re-attention (§1.0). \textbf{The SEKA-vs-ours comparison separates by task regime}: SEKA for single-tool (Subtask1, parity), Q-coverage for multi-tool (Subtask4, dominant).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Ontology-privileged subspace stability (Cor 6.9.6, §3.3)}. On MetaTool Subtask4 (N=497, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), real $B_\mathrm{ont}$ at $\alpha=0.3$ maintains F1 = 0.685 while random and feature-shuffled $B_\mathrm{ont}$ at identical magnitude collapse to F1 = 0.000 — \textbf{direction-specificity gap +68.5pp}. Formalized as: $\mathrm{span}(B_\mathrm{ont})$ is the unique rank-$R$ K-perturbation subspace with KL divergence $O(\alpha^2)$ (on-manifold); off-manifold perturbations cross the FC-emission boundary at $\alpha \geq \alpha^*$. Effective-magnitude diagnostic (§3.3.2) confirms real $B_\mathrm{ont}$ produces \emph{larger} attention-logit perturbation (1.56×–2.13×) than controls, ruling out the "safe because small-effective-magnitude" alternative. \textbf{Stability, not accuracy} — the K-bias direction does not produce accuracy lift on multi-tool; its value is the formal characterization of $B_\mathrm{ont}$ as geometrically privileged.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Categorical compression via the same ontology axis (Thm 6.13 + 6.18, §3.5 + §3.6.2)}. The facet basis doubles as a quantization axis. OCQ (1-bit categorical on $B_\mathrm{ont}$ + 2-bit asymmetric residual) at 1.81 avg bits beats KIVI at 2.00 bits by **$-4.37$ PPL** on Qwen2.5-7B WT2; cross-over at 4-bit predicted by Cor 6.13.5 and observed. Attention-weighted bit allocation (Thm 6.18) minimizes Thm 6.1 attention-output distortion at any bit budget via reverse water-filling on $\pi(t,f)\sigma_f^2$. Predicted: $-2.5$ PPL over uniform OCQ at 1.81 avg bits.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Single-basis tri-role sufficiency (Thm 6.19, §3.6.3)}. The \emph{same} per-head $B_\mathrm{ont}$ — constructed \emph{once} from facet annotations — simultaneously parameterizes Q-steering accuracy (multi-tool), K-stability diagnostic, and K-compression, with zero asymptotic overhead (Cor 6.19.2). The three roles are \emph{orthogonal} (Q-side vs K-side intervention sites, accuracy vs stability vs compression objectives), yet share a single geometric object — a parsimony result absent from prior steering+compression literature.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Theorem 6.1: per-sample attention-weighted bound (§3.1)}. $\mathbb E_q\|\hat o - o\|^2 \le 2\mathbb E[\mathrm{qaMSE}\cdot \mathrm{Var}_s V] + C_1\rho^4$. The bound underpins all three roles in contribution 4. Verified on Qwen2.5-7B L=13 $\alpha=0.3$: bound\_pass\_rate 2800/2800, median LHS/RHS ratio $2.36\times 10^{-8}$ (loose but directionally informative; §F.1).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Plan-success predictor via cumulative stability (Thm 6.20, §5.10.2)} — \emph{deployment-relevant}. Per-step ontology stability $\varepsilon_{q_t} = \|B_\mathrm{ont}^\top q_t\|^2 / \|q_t\|^2$ predicts multi-step plan failure. Verified at smoke (N=100, Qwen Subtask4): unstratified AUROC = 0.976 [0.947, 1.000]; length-exact-constant rebuttal at $n_\mathrm{steps}=29$ (4 fail vs 4 success, perfectly separable at $\varepsilon^* = 0.14$) addresses generation-length confound. Full τ²-bench retail multi-turn validation queued.
\end{enumerate}
