\section{2. Related Work}
We organize prior work into three threads — \textbf{(A) inference-time activation/attention steering}, \textbf{(B) KV-cache compression}, \textbf{(C) attention bounds and theory} — and a fourth on \textbf{(D) tool-use evaluation}.


\subsection{2.1 Activation / attention steering}

The literature spans residual-stream interventions, attention-output and attention-map interventions, and (most recently) K-side spectral interventions. We list each method by intervention site and direction-source, with explicit positioning of our contribution.


\textbf{Residual-stream additive interventions.}


\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Activation Addition (ActAdd)} (Turner et al. 2023, arXiv:2308.10248) — single learned vector added to residual stream, all positions.
\item \textbf{Representation Engineering (RepE)} (Zou et al. 2023, arXiv:2310.01405) — superset framework for residual-stream steering across many concepts (sentiment, truth, refusal). Subramani et al. 2022 (Latent Steering Vectors) is the academic origin.
\item \textbf{Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA)} (Rimsky et al. 2024, ACL; arXiv:2312.06681) — mean-difference of multiple-choice contrastive pair activations at residual stream layer. Llama-2-7B-Chat L=13 optimal, 7 behaviors. Code: \texttt{nrimsky/CAA}.
\item \textbf{Conceptors} (Yan et al. 2024, arXiv:2410.16314) — improved addition-based steering using conceptors.
\item \textbf{SAE-Trained Steering (SAE-TS)} (Chalnev 2024, arXiv:2411.02193) — Sparse Auto-Encoder feature directions; first quantitative proof that decoder direction ≠ causal direction (Golden Gate Claude failure mechanism).
\item \textbf{KL-then-Steer (KTS)} (Stickland et al. 2024, arXiv:2406.15518) — adds forward-KL fine-tuning loss to mitigate steering side effects.
\item \textbf{ASA (Adaptive Steering for Activation)} (Wang et al. 2026, arXiv:2602.04935) — single-layer residual stream Mixture-of-Vectors with router + ternary gate, last-prompt-token only. MTU-Bench Qwen2.5-1.5B: F1 0.18→0.50 with FPR 0.15→0.05 at α=4.0 L=18.
\item \textbf{Activation Steering with Feedback Controller} (2025, arXiv:2510.04309) — runtime closed-loop control.
\item \textbf{AdaActSteer} (Web Conf 2025) — adaptive truthfulness-improving steering for diverse hallucination categories.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Per-head attention-output interventions.}


\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Inference-Time Intervention (ITI)} (Li et al. 2023, NeurIPS; arXiv:2306.03341) — bias on \texttt{o\_proj} input (attention-output, post-softmax-weighted V, pre-W\_O). Equivalent to constant W\_O bias; attention pattern \emph{untouched}. Llama-7B optimal K=48/1024 heads, α=15. TruthfulQA True×Info 30.5 → 43.5. Code: \texttt{likenneth/honest\_llama}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Attention-map / attention-score interventions.}


\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{PASTA} (Zhang et al. 2024, ICLR; arXiv:2311.02262) — post-softmax row reweighting for user-marked tokens, intersection of top-k heads from multi-task profiling. Llama-7B 50–150 heads, α=0.01 default. Code: \texttt{QingruZhang/PASTA}.
\item \textbf{GUIDE / InstABoost / Spotlight} (2024–2025, arXiv:2409.19001 / 2506.13734 / 2505.12025) — attention-score bias family.
\item \textbf{Fact Grounded Attention (FGA)} (Gupta 2025, arXiv:2509.25252) — pre-softmax attention bias from external fact KB (137 entities × 12 attributes). Layer 20–27 of Llama-3.2-3B; 6.3\% → 99.7\% on 1107 spec QA. Code: \texttt{ayushgupta4897/FGA}. \emph{Closest prior art to ontology-guided attention}; explicitly invites hierarchical/compositional fact representations as future work (§6.2.1) — exactly our $B_{\mathrm{ont}}$ direction.
\item \textbf{Focus Directions} (Zhu et al. 2025, arXiv:2503.23306) — additive K AND Q bias at top-k contextual heads (gradient-trained directions); Llama-3.2-3B layers 8–18, α=0.3, top-20 heads of 672. HELMET benchmark only. Does NOT ablate K-only vs Q-only.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{K-side spectral interventions} (most directly comparable to our work).


\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{SEKA} (Li et al., arXiv:2603.01281, ICLR 2026) — spectral editing of K via $k' = k + \tfrac12 (g^+ P^+ k + g^- P^- k)$ where $P^\pm$ are top-k singular-vector projections from contrastive cross-covariance. Pre-softmax, K-only, scalar gains per (task, model). Steer-mask over user-marked tokens (\texttt{**...**} markers). Not ontology-derived. Benchmarks: CounterFact, BiasBios, Pronouns, Lost-in-Middle. Models: Qwen3 + Gemma3. Code: \texttt{waylonli/SEKA}. \emph{Most consequential prior art for our K-side stability claim} — operator family near-identical, our differentiation is (i) ontology-derived basis vs contrastive SVD, (ii) per-facet $B_f$ decomposition, (iii) Cor 6.9.6 distributional KL bound.
\item \textbf{AdaSEKA} (Kim et al. 2026) — query-adaptive SEKA: dynamic projection $P_{\mathrm{dyn}} = \sum_m \alpha_m U_m U_m^\top$ where $\alpha_m$ from SVD-aligned routing on last prompt token. K-side, single-per-query. Source in \texttt{external/SEKA/src/model/adaptive\_seka\_llm.py}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Positioning of our work (v3, axis-separation).} Our contribution and prior work sit on \textbf{different axes of the tool-selection regime}:


\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{SEKA / AdaSEKA / Focus Directions} are \emph{stationary K-side} operators designed for single-selection: a single contrastive-SVD projection $P$ is applied uniformly to every decoding step. This works well when one correct emission is required (MetaTool Subtask1, CounterFact, BiasBios — the benchmarks on which SEKA was originally demonstrated). Under autoregressive generation with multi-step emission (MetaTool Subtask4), a stationary K-bias that concentrates attention on one facet cluster at step 1 continues to do so at step 2, preventing emission of the second-tool's facet. This is a \emph{structural limitation}, not a hyperparameter matter: see §1.0 for the formal argument, §5.5 for the empirical pattern ($-4.6$pp our K-bias α=0.3, $-7.95$pp SEKA amp=1.0 on Llama Subtask4).
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Our step-adaptive Q-coverage} $\Delta_Q^{(t)} = -\beta \sum_{s<t} P_{f_s} q_t$ is a \emph{multi-selection} operator. At step $t$, facets already emitted are \emph{subtracted} from the query, redirecting attention to unused facets. This requires: (a) a facet decomposition of the ontology subspace $B_\mathrm{ont} = [B_{f_1}, \ldots, B_{f_F}]$ (not present in SEKA's contrastive-SVD construction), (b) emission tracking across decoding steps to maintain $\{f_s\}_{s<t}$ (a trivial $O(1)$ state that stationary operators lack), (c) Q-side intervention site (distinct from all K-side spectral methods). No prior work combines these three. We do \emph{not} compete with SEKA on Subtask1 (the parity regime check); we introduce the Subtask4 multi-selection axis.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Residual-stream methods} (ActAdd, CAA, RepE, Conceptors, SAE-TS, ASA) act at the layer output, \emph{not} at Q before scoring. Our Q-coverage is attention-channel-specific: $\Delta_Q^{(t)}$ modifies only $q$, leaving residual stream untouched, enabling surgical tool-selection steering without affecting general token distribution. A rank-1 CAA-on-$B_\mathrm{ont}$ baseline on Subtask4 achieves $+1.6$pp F1 (equivalent to Q-coverage within SE), confirming the \emph{basis} is load-bearing while the \emph{mechanism} (residual vs Q) is largely interchangeable on this benchmark.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Attention-map interventions} (PASTA, GUIDE, InstABoost, Spotlight, FGA) reweight post-softmax attention rows. These \emph{cannot} produce step-adaptive coverage because the post-softmax reweighting does not know the token emission history across steps; they face the same multi-selection structural barrier as stationary K-bias.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Core differentiation from prior art}:


\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Intervention site + step-adaptivity}: Q-side, per-step (unique; all prior methods stationary).
\item \textbf{Facet-block decomposition of the direction basis} ($B_\mathrm{ont} = \bigoplus_f B_f$, rank $R = \sum_f r_f$): no prior method has per-facet structure; SEKA's contrastive SVD gives a monolithic $P$.
\item \textbf{Ontology as direction source} (training-free DeepSeek-V3 classification of tools) vs contrastive paired data (CAA, SEKA), gradient training (Focus, ITI), or fact KB (FGA).
\item \textbf{Cor 6.9.6 distributional KL bound}: formal stability characterization; no prior K-side / Q-side method has a distributional bound.
\item \textbf{Single-basis tri-role sufficiency (Thm 6.19)}: the same $B_\mathrm{ont}$ simultaneously parameterizes Q-steering accuracy (multi-tool), K-stability diagnostic, and K-compression.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{2.2 KV-cache compression}

We organize into five families.


\textbf{Per-channel quantization.}


\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{KIVI} (Liu et al. 2024, ICML; arXiv:2402.02750) — 2-bit per-channel asymmetric K-quant + per-token V-quant; 2.35×–3.47× throughput on LLaMA/Falcon/Mistral.
\item \textbf{KVQuant} (Hooper et al. 2024, NeurIPS; arXiv:2401.18079) — per-channel uniform + outlier preservation + pre-RoPE; 10M context length on LLaMA-7B.
\item \textbf{AsymKV} — asymmetric K vs V bit-allocation.
\item \textbf{KIVI-style turboquant baselines} (internal, 2026-04-01) — used as our internal reference.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Token eviction / sequence-axis.}


\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{H2O (Heavy-Hitter Oracle)} (Zhang et al. 2024, NeurIPS; arXiv:2306.14048) — dynamic eviction balancing recent + heavy-hitter tokens.
\item \textbf{StreamingLLM} (Xiao et al. 2024, ICLR; arXiv:2309.17453) — attention sinks preservation.
\item \textbf{SnapKV} (Li et al. 2024, NeurIPS; arXiv:2404.14469) — observation-window-based pattern.
\item \textbf{DynamicKV} (2025, ACL Findings) — task-aware compression.
\item \textbf{GEAR} — pyramidal eviction + low-rank.
\item These are \emph{orthogonal} to our feature-axis approach; eviction methods stack on top of any feature-axis quantizer including ours.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Rotation-based feature-axis quantization.}


\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{TurboQuant} (Pourreza et al. 2024, arXiv:2406.03482) — random orthogonal rotation + Lloyd-Max scalar codebook; baseline in our retrospective (§5.9.2).
\item \textbf{QuaRot} (Ashkboos et al. 2024, NeurIPS; arXiv:2404.00456) — fixed Hadamard rotations to disperse outliers; preprocesses W/A/KV uniformly; >99\% of fp16 accuracy at 4-bit; online Hadamard for KV cache.
\item \textbf{SpinQuant} (Liu et al. 2024, arXiv:2405.16406) — learned rotation matrices via Cayley optimization; outperforms QuaRot on hard-to-quantize models (LLaMA-3 8B), reduces gap to fp16 by up to 45.1\%.
\item \textbf{PolarQuant} — polar-coordinate quantization.
\item \emph{Critical empirical observation} (\texttt{reports/EXPERIMENT\_REPORT\_COMPREHENSIVE\_2026-04-09.md}): on Mistral-7B WT2 49K test, PCA vs Random differs only by 0.07\% (3-bit) / 0.9\% (2-bit). The \emph{quality of the rotation} therefore is not where the leverage lies. Our $B_{\mathrm{ont}}$ exploits a \emph{different axis} of variation: not "good rotation for Gaussian features" but \emph{categorical-channel separation} respecting (H-cat).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Low-rank projection / DP-bit allocation.}


\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{ThinK} — learned PCA on K with adaptive rank.
\item \textbf{LESS} — learned subspace + recall.
\item \textbf{KVCompress} — column pruning.
\item \textbf{KVTC} (NVIDIA, ICLR 2026; OnlyTerp/kvtc) — per-layer PCA + DP-optimal bit allocation per component (variance-based) + DEFLATE/LZMA2 entropy coding; up to 20× compression on Qwen2.5-7B / Qwen3.5-27B / LLaMA-3.1-8B. Most direct compression baseline; our internal Llama-3.1-8B 2-bit retrospective shows \emph{per-head} PCA beats \emph{shared} PCA (KVTC-style) by 46.3\% (10.14 vs 18.87 PPL) — our $B_{(\ell,h)}$ is per-(layer, head) by construction.
\item \textbf{MiniKV} (ACL Findings 2025) — pushes 2-bit limits.
\item \textbf{ChunkKV} — semantic-preserving chunk-level compression.
\item \textbf{CodeComp} (2026, arXiv:2604.10235) — structural compression for agentic coding.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Hybrid / sequence-feature stack.}


\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{KVSink} (Su, COLM 2025; arXiv:2508.04257) — preserve sink token positions in fp16 while compressing rest. Code unreleased. Sequence-axis row-selection, \emph{orthogonal to our column-axis ontology}. Stackable with OCQ; our 2×2 ablation predicts combined > max(individual).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Lloyd-vs-Uniform paradox} (\texttt{reports/EXPERIMENT\_REPORT\_COMPREHENSIVE\_2026-04-09.md}, internal): Lloyd-Max scalar quantization, despite reducing reconstruction MSE by 74\%, \emph{increased} PPL in 9/9 settings (3 models × 3 bit-widths). This empirical separation between MSE and downstream quality is what motivates our Thm 6.1 attention-output bound as the correct optimization target.


\textbf{Surveys}: Tang et al. 2024 "KV Cache Compression for Inference Efficiency in LLMs: A Review" (arXiv:2508.06297), Liu et al. 2025 (arXiv:2603.20397) cover the field comprehensively. NVIDIA's \texttt{kvpress} library implements many of these methods uniformly.


\textbf{Positioning of our work.} Our Thm 6.13 / 6.18 / 6.19 occupy a different axis from all five families: (i) \emph{categorical} (ontology-derived) rather than Gaussian (PCA, KVTC) decorrelation; (ii) \emph{attention-output distortion} (Thm 6.1) rather than reconstruction-MSE objective; (iii) the same basis $B_{\mathrm{ont}}$ simultaneously parameterizes inference-time steering Pareto-optimality (Thm 6.19) — a coupling no prior compression work considers. Detailed empirical comparison in §5.9.1 (KVTC) and §5.9.2 (FOKVQ-PCA retrospective).


\subsection{2.3 Attention bounds and theory}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Kim–Papyan–Donoho} (NeurIPS 2021) — softmax-attention Lipschitz constants; basis of our Thm 6.1 quartic remainder term.
\item \textbf{Zhang–Kumar} (2023) — token-mixing perturbation bounds.
\item No prior work gives a \emph{per-query, per-head} attention-output bound with leading term $\mathrm{qaMSE} \cdot \mathrm{Var}_s[V]$ (our Thm 6.1).
\item Mode-A/B/C attention regimes (Park et al. 2024) — basis for our Mode-A/B/C analysis (§3.2 and Appendix B.6).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{2.6 Threat analysis — full-text comparison with 4 most threatening recent works}

We did full-text reads of the 4 most threatening prior works and document our differentiation:


\subsubsection{2.6.1 SEKA (Li et al., ICLR 2026, arXiv:2603.01281)}

\textbf{Mechanism (Eq 2 of their paper)}: $\Omega^\pm = h^\top h^\pm / n$ (cross-covariance from contrastive prompt pairs), SVD: $\Omega^\pm = U^\pm S^\pm V^{\pm \top}$, then $$P^+_{\ell,h} = U^+_{:,:k^+} (U^+_{:,:k^+})^\top, \quad P^-_{\ell,h} = U^-_{:,k^-:} (U^-_{:,k^-:})^\top$$ Steering: $k' = k + g P k$ — \emph{operator form identical to our K-bias}.


\textbf{SEKA scope}:


\begin{itemize}
\item Benchmarks: CounterFact (ES/PS), BiasInBios, Pronouns. \textbf{No multi-tool, no function-calling}.
\item Models: Qwen3-\{4B,8B,14B\}, Gemma3-\{4B,12B,27B\}. \emph{No Llama, no Mistral, no Qwen2.5}.
\item Hyperparameter: γ (variance retention threshold), single g per (task, model).
\item Steer-mask: tokens between \texttt{**...**} markers.
\item FlashAttention compatible; latency +0.03s vs PASTA's +1.03s.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{SEKA limitations} (admitted or implicit):


\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{No theoretical bound} (geometric interpretation only).
\item \emph{No per-direction gains} — single g+ and g- per (task, model).
\item \emph{No multi-direction / per-facet decomposition} — just top-k+ vs bottom-k-.
\item \emph{Direction source = task-specific contrastive pairs} (CounterFact / BiasBios).
\item \emph{Task-routing} in AdaSEKA, not facet-composition.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Our differentiation} (4 dimensions, each falsifiable):


\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{X | X | X}
\toprule
Direction source & task-contrastive SVD & ontology annotation (DeepSeek-V3, training-free) \\
\midrule
Decomposition & top-k+/bottom-k- (2 directions) & per-facet $B_f$ blocks (4 facets, rank-$R = \sum r_f$) \\
Theory & geometric interpretation & Thm 6.1 + Cor 6.7-6.13 + Cor 6.9.6 distributional KL bound \\
Cross-domain coupling & none & Thm 6.19 steering<->compression Pareto \\
Multi-tool eval & absent & full Subtask4 N=497 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\textbf{Threat verdict}: SEKA is the most direct prior. Operator form match (k+gPk) is \emph{not} novelty for us. Our 4 differentiators above must each be verified empirically. \emph{SEKA-on-MetaTool-Subtask4 is the critical missing comparison} (eval in progress, §5.5.3.1). If SEKA underperforms on multi-tool (cf. our Cor 9.6 prediction that Subtask4 is fundamentally hard for K-side), our differentiation is preserved.


\subsubsection{2.6.2 FGA — Fact Grounded Attention (Gupta 2025, arXiv:2509.25252)}

\textbf{Mechanism (Eq 5, 7 of their paper)}: $$G = B_{qf} \cdot A \in \mathbb R^{L \times L}, \quad S_{\mathrm{FGA}} = S + \alpha \odot G$$ where $A \in \{0,1\}^{M \times L}$ is binary entity-to-token assignment, $B_{qf} = QK_{\mathrm{fact}}^\top / \sqrt{d_k}$ is query-fact affinity (learned $W_K \approx 2.1$M params).


\textbf{FGA scope}:


\begin{itemize}
\item Benchmark: 1107 spec QA (smartphones, laptops, EVs) + public benchmarks.
\item Layer 20–27 of Llama-3.2-3B (deep layers only).
\item Flat KB: 137 entities × 12 attributes — \emph{no hierarchy}.
\item Fine-tuning: 99.7\% accuracy (zero-shot 87.1\%, baseline 6.3\%).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{FGA explicit limitations} (their §6.2.1, 6.2.3):


\begin{itemize}
\item "FGA requires structured facts. Procedural knowledge, implicit reasoning... remain challenging."
\item "Future work should explore \textbf{hierarchical and compositional fact representations}." ← *our $B_{\mathrm{ont}}$ is exactly this future-work direction they invite*
\item "Multi hop Reasoning: FGA currently handles single fact queries well but \textbf{struggles with complex reasoning requiring multiple facts}. \textbf{Compositional grounding mechanisms are a promising direction}." ← \emph{multi-tool function-calling = multiple facts; our work fills this gap}
\end{itemize}

\midrule

Hierarchy & flat (137 × 12) & per-(layer, head, facet) $B_f$ block \\

\textbf{Threat verdict}: FGA's Section 6.2 \emph{literally invites our contribution} ("hierarchical/compositional fact representations"). Our $B_{\mathrm{ont}}$ realizes exactly the future-work direction FGA suggests but cannot implement (their representation is flat). Differentiation safe; we should cite FGA prominently as motivation in §1.


\subsubsection{2.6.3 Focus Directions (Zhu et al. 2025, arXiv:2503.23306)}

\textbf{Mechanism (Eq 5 of their paper)}: $$W = \mathrm{softmax}\left(\frac{(Q + \alpha d_Q)(K + \alpha d_K)^\top}{\sqrt F}\right)$$


\textbf{Focus Directions scope}:


\begin{itemize}
\item $d_K, d_Q$ trained via gradient descent (AdamW, lr=10\^\{\}-³, 10 epochs) on Multi-Document QA.
\item Llama-3.2-3B, layers 8–18, top-20 contextual heads of 672.
\item $\alpha \in \{−0.2, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5\}$, optimal $\alpha = 0.3$.
\item HELMET benchmark only (NQ, TriviaQA, HotpotQA, PopQA, MS MARCO, ...).
\item \emph{No K-only vs Q-only ablation} — always joint K+Q.
\item Only 1 future-work line ("converge across tasks that share the same context length").
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{2.6.4 KVTC (NVIDIA, ICLR 2026)}

\textbf{Mechanism}: PCA basis (per-layer) + DP-optimal bit allocation per component (variance-based) + DEFLATE/LZMA2 entropy coding.


\textbf{Scope}: up to 20× compression on Qwen2.5-7B / Qwen3.5-27B / LLaMA-3.1-8B; cosine similarity 0.981–0.9999.


\textbf{KVTC limitations}:


\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{No theoretical bound} (empirical cosine only).
\item \emph{Reconstruction MSE objective} (not attention-output distortion).
\item \emph{Shared (per-layer) PCA}, not per-head — our internal data shows per-head improves Llama 2-bit by 46.3\% (§5.9.2).
\item \emph{Compression-only}, no steering coupling.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Our differentiation} (already documented §5.9.1):

\toprule
Decorrelation & PCA (Gaussian) & categorical (H-cat) \\
\midrule

Per-head & shared per-layer & per-(layer, head) by construction \\
Theory & none & Thm 6.13 + Thm 6.19 + cross-over Cor 6.13.5 \\

Raw compression & 20× & 7.77× standalone (orthogonal-stackable to KVTC entropy coding) \\

\textbf{Threat verdict}: KVTC wins on raw bit-ratio. We position as theory + cross-domain coupling, not raw-compression competition. KVTC's entropy coding is \emph{orthogonal-stackable} to ours; their PCA basis is \emph{replaceable} with our $B_{\mathrm{ont}}$. \emph{Stack of OCQ + DP-allocation + DEFLATE} would surpass either alone. Differentiation safe via complementarity narrative (§5.9.1).
