\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Setup}

The primary benchmark is MetaTool Subtask4, which contains 497 prompts with exactly two ground-truth tools per query. We report macro F1, Exact match, and Jaccard. The main models are Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct \citep{qwen2024qwen25,grattafiori2024llama3}. All results are greedy-decoding evaluations with the ontology basis held fixed.

\subsection{Main result: Q-side positive, K-side negative}

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} and Figure~\ref{fig:main-results} summarize the main result. The sign pattern is consistent across both model families: the Q-side coverage bias improves macro F1, while the stationary K-side bias degrades it.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Main Subtask4 results. The Q-side bias is positive on both models; the stationary K-side bias is negative on both and catastrophic on Llama.}
\label{tab:main-results}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llccc@{}}
\toprule
Model & Method & F1 & Exact & Jaccard \\
\midrule
Qwen2.5-7B-Inst & No steer & 0.7307 & 0.5252 & 0.6673 \\
Qwen2.5-7B-Inst & Q-bias $\beta=-0.1$ & \textbf{0.7471} & \textbf{0.5272} & \textbf{0.6791} \\
Qwen2.5-7B-Inst & K-bias $\alpha=0.3$ & 0.6850 & 0.4728 & 0.6194 \\
\midrule
Llama-3.1-8B-Inst & No steer & 0.6227 & 0.5030 & 0.5872 \\
Llama-3.1-8B-Inst & Q-bias $\beta=-0.1$ & \textbf{0.6271} & \textbf{0.5070} & \textbf{0.5919} \\
Llama-3.1-8B-Inst & K-bias $\alpha=0.3$ & 0.3105 & 0.2616 & 0.2951 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_main_subtask4_results.pdf}
\caption{Subtask4 macro F1 on the two primary models. The left panel also includes Qwen null controls.}
\label{fig:main-results}
\end{figure}

The absolute gain is modest: +1.64pp on Qwen and +0.44pp on Llama. That is not enough to justify a sweeping accuracy claim. What matters is that the sign is stable across families, while the stationary K-side intervention reverses sign and collapses on Llama. The paper therefore argues for a \emph{directional} conclusion: if the goal is multi-tool recovery, query-space suppression is the useful axis and stationary key-space amplification is not.

\subsection{Ontology specificity is decisive}

The stronger result is not the headline gain but the null-control behavior. Table~\ref{tab:null-controls} shows that replacing the ontology basis with a feature-shuffled or random basis removes the Q-side gain and completely destroys K-side stability.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Qwen Subtask4 null controls. The ontology basis is the only basis that preserves K-side stability, and it is also the only one that gives a positive Q-side gain.}
\label{tab:null-controls}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
Method & Real basis & Feature-shuffled & Random \\
\midrule
Q-bias $\beta=-0.1$ F1 & \textbf{0.7471} & 0.7254 & 0.7068 \\
K-bias $\alpha=0.3$ F1 & \textbf{0.6850} & 0.0000 & 0.0000 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

This is the most convincing empirical point in the paper. A generic low-rank perturbation is not enough. Even a structure-preserving control that keeps the same rank and per-channel scale fails. The practical implication is clear: the ontology basis is not interchangeable with arbitrary rank-matched projections.

\subsection{The useful regime is narrow}

Figure~\ref{fig:ablations} shows two ablations on Qwen Subtask4. The Q-only sweep peaks at $\beta=-0.1$ among the evaluated values. A second sweep adds a small K-bias on top of the Q-side operator; only $\alpha_K \in [0.025, 0.05]$ improves on the Q-only setting.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_qbias_ablations.pdf}
\caption{Q-bias and small-$\alpha_K$ ablations on Qwen Subtask4. Both curves are sharply localized.}
\label{fig:ablations}
\end{figure}

This narrow operating region matters for interpretation. The result is not ``more bias is better.'' It is ``a small negative Q-side projection helps, and almost everything else degrades.'' That pattern is consistent with a coverage effect rather than a coarse confidence boost.

\subsection{Stability is not explained by smaller perturbation}

One easy objection is that the real ontology basis may simply be safer because it perturbs less than the controls. Figure~\ref{fig:stability-bound} rejects that explanation. The real basis produces the \emph{largest} effective attention perturbation magnitude, yet it is still the only stable K-side direction.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_stability_and_bound.pdf}
\caption{Two diagnostics. Left: the real ontology perturbation is larger than the null controls. Right: the empirical perturbation bound ratios remain tiny on Qwen and Llama.}
\label{fig:stability-bound}
\end{figure}

This is why the null-control result is meaningful. The real basis does not win by being weak. It wins by being aligned with a specific stable subspace. The same figure also shows that the bound ratios remain very small on both model families.

\subsection{Secondary evidence}

The repository also contains single-tool results on MetaTool Subtask1 and a smoke predictor based on query-space stability. We do not use them as main claims here. They are useful as consistency checks, but the current paper is strongest when it stays centered on multi-tool selection, ontology specificity, and the contrast between query-side and stationary key-side interventions.
