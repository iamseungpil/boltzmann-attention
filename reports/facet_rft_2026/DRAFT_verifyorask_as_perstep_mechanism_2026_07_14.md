## EN draft (paper `what_scale_buys` — method/related-work)

**Long-horizon reliability as a product, and the per-step mechanism it requires.**
A long-horizon agent succeeds only if a long conjunction of per-step judgments all hold, so end-to-end success decays as the product of per-step reliabilities and collapses even at high single-step accuracy (H_s(p)=⌈ln s / ln p⌉; Sinha & Geiping, arXiv:2509.09677). A now-standard family of methods attacks this by **decomposition**: cutting the task into minimal, context-isolated subtasks so that a local error cannot propagate (Maximal Agentic Decomposition, MAKER; Task-Decoupled Planning, arXiv:2601.07577; Tree/Graph-of-Thoughts, arXiv:2305.10601). Decomposition, however, only reshapes the chain; it still requires a **per-step correctness mechanism** to keep each conjunct reliable. Existing methods supply this with *statistical* or *self-evaluative* signals — first-to-ahead-by-k **voting** (MAKER), which costs k× samples per step and remains probabilistic, or LLM **self-evaluation** of candidate thoughts (ToT/GoT), which is known to be unreliable: intrinsic self-correction without external feedback does not improve, and can degrade, model outputs (Huang et al., arXiv:2310.01798; Kamoi et al., TACL 2024).

**Our contribution: replace the per-step mechanism with decidability-routed verify-or-ASK.**
We adopt the decomposition substrate as-is and contribute the per-step correctness mechanism it lacks. The key observation is that the statistical mechanism decomposition relies on — resampling and majority voting — **extracts signal from variance**, and is therefore *vacuous on the errors that dominate our residual*. Both canonical voting results are explicit about this precondition. Self-consistency "leverages the intuition that a complex reasoning problem typically admits multiple different ways of thinking leading to its unique correct answer" (Wang et al., arXiv:2203.11171) — i.e. diverse paths converge on the correct answer as the mode. MAKER's million-step, zero-error result rests on "the statistical power of independent samples from a stochastic process (here an LLM)" and, for its analysis, "assumed that the errors are i.i.d. across steps" (§3.2); crucially the authors place the case we target outside their reach, tying it to model-training side-effects: "Such strange behaviors for particular inputs are well-known side-effects of LLM training, and dealing with such correlated errors is an open foundational problem in machine learning" (§5) (arXiv:2511.09030). Our residual is exactly that case: the errors are *systematic* (a shared prior or absent information), so every resample returns the **same** wrong answer and the wrong answer is the mode. We measured this directly — on the intent-selection / reference-matching residual, self-consistency yields **+0%** with **8-of-8 unanimous identical errors** (whereas serial CoT yields +4%); zero variance leaves resampling nothing to aggregate, and the unanimity simultaneously defeats any disagreement-flag. This matches the theoretical bound that parallel voting cannot add serial-computation depth (Mirtaheri et al., arXiv:2505.21825). Voting is thus not a weaker-but-usable baseline on this residual; on the regime the decomposition literature itself declares open, it is settled-negative.

Given a mechanism that cannot help by aggregation, only two moves remain, and they are exactly the two sides of the decidability split. We classify each subtask as *decidable* — checkable from information already in context — or *non-decidable* — requiring information the model cannot possess. For decidable steps we **change the computation**: deterministic verification injects a prior-independent, exact check that a systematic bias cannot survive — precisely what voting (which re-runs the same biased computation) cannot do. For non-decidable steps, no internal mechanism can help — voting merely amplifies a confident guess, the regime where intrinsic self-correction provably fails (Huang et al., arXiv:2310.01798) — so we **ask the user**, supplying the external information that is definitionally the only thing that can resolve the step. Note this cleanly bounds where decomposition's own voting *does* work: MAKER-style voting beats the exponential on tasks with **stochastic, high-variance** per-step errors (e.g. mechanical Towers-of-Hanoi steps); it is our **systematic, zero-variance** residual where voting collapses and verify-or-ASK is the only valid mechanism.

**Bounding the ASK budget with an information-theoretic floor.**
Routing every non-decidable conjunct to a question risks a second failure mode — dialogue inflation leading the user to abandon the task. We bound this with the irreducible information the user must supply, an information-theoretic floor H_min (per dispute type, banking: 4.27 bits for debit, 2.60 for credit; a full interaction ≈ 11–18 bits ≈ 5–7 questions), and fold the *effective* number of questions down toward that floor: fields determined by others are **derived** rather than re-asked (≈18 zero-information fields), fields with a dominant value take a **default** (e.g. police-report ≈ always false), and the remainder are ordered by value of information. Because H_min is a structural property of the task, this residual is a **non-scaling** lever: a larger base model does not shrink the bits the user alone can provide, which is why a small model equipped with this deterministic mechanism can close the gap to a frontier model on the compliance/horizon residual.

**Scope and what remains to measure.**
The negative result for voting on the systematic residual is already established (self-consistency +0%, 8-of-8 unanimous, graded [M]); we do not re-litigate it. What remains open is the *validity of the replacement*, not a cost comparison. Deterministic verification helps only on the *correctly-formalizable* decidable subset — where the check is mis-formalized, verification injects a **new** systematic error (false confidence) rather than correcting one, so the intervention must be evaluated for spurious-action harm (Δspurious ≤ 0), not assumed net-positive. Likewise, DERIVE presumes its derivation mechanism holds (noisy fields, e.g. a messy card suffix, raise the effective H_min). We therefore report the composition as three claims with distinct evidence status: (i) voting/self-consistency is vacuous on the systematic residual — **settled [M]**; (ii) on the correctly-formalizable decidable subset, deterministic verify closes steps that voting cannot, at Δspurious ≤ 0 — **to measure**; (iii) the ASK budget folds to a measured information-theoretic floor via DERIVE/DEFAULT/VOI — **H_min settled [S], end-to-end fold to measure**.
