> Full-text deep-read (mechanism + tables + limitations, not abstracts) of the priority diversity/decoding/alignment-collapse papers in `research_diversity_2026_06_14.md`.
> STRICT citation discipline: every quote below was pulled from the arXiv abstract page and/or the ar5iv HTML full-text on 2026-06-13/14. Where the abstract and a body-render disagreed, both are reported and the discrepancy flagged. Quotes are verbatim within the per-quote length cap; paraphrase is marked as such.
> Our framework under test: `scripts/distill/SELECTOR_DESIGN.md` §5(⑸)/§7 + TB results §8.9f/§8.9g/§8.9h. Core claim being audited: **"useful diversity = different-AND-right (D-oracle = oracle(pool) − max single-policy), NOT pairwise-distance"** — because P-lora's pairwise-distance diversity 0.1535 (≈10× single-policy) came with Δoracle ≈ +0.0035 ("different-wrong jams consensus"), so the unified-pool selection regressed (recall 61%→40%).

---

## 0. Headline verdict (front-loaded)

1. **The literature SUPPORTS the D-oracle-not-pairwise-distance correction — but mostly by *implication and assumption*, not by an explicit theorem that names the distinction.** The strongest support is structural: every paper that proves diversity helps test-time selection either (a) selects with a **ground-truth verifier / oracle reward** (so "right" is enforced by construction), or (b) assumes per-sample correctness is retained, or (c) explicitly shows the gain **vanishes under majority voting** (consensus). None of them show raw pairwise-distance diversity helping a gold-free consensus-style selector — which is precisely the regime where our unified pool failed. So our autopsy fills a genuinely under-stated gap rather than contradicting prior work.
2. **DivPO/GEM are worth the v4 retrain — conditionally.** Both are *quality-gated* diversity methods (DivPO: chosen = rare AND high-quality; GEM: diversity that **translates to BoN gains** in their own tables, +7.6 pts HumanEval Pass@100). Their mechanism is exactly the "different-AND-right" we need, and GEM's win is measured on test-time scaling (our use-case), not just intrinsic diversity. The risk is that their gains are demonstrated on **open-ended chat/creative + code**, not on narrow tool-DAG output where the answer space is small — so the v4 gate must remain "k0 strength ∧ pool D-oracle ≥ baseline," not "pairwise diversity up."
3. **One prior-report over-claim found + one now-resolvable lead.** The prior report's gloss that 2502.11027 carries a *mechanistic* "different-wrong jams consensus / errors reinforce don't cancel" caveat is **NOT supported by the paper body** — that was a fast-model paraphrase artifact; the paper's actual majority-voting statement is a bare one-liner ("under majority voting, diversity may vanish") with the headline gains coming from **oracle/verifier BoN**. Separately, the suspect future-dated **2603.19146 (D5P4)** — listed as unverified Ⓢ-only — **does now resolve** to a real paper.

## 1. Priority papers — claim / FIT / ERROR / one-line

### 1.1 — Diversity → BoN gain, causal: 2502.11027 (v4, 2025-12-19)
**"On the Effect of Sampling Diversity in Scaling LLM Inference"** — Tianchun Wang, Zichuan Liu, Yuanzhou Chen, Jonathan Light, Weiyang Liu, Haifeng Chen, Xiang Zhang, Wei Cheng.

**1. Claim/mechanism (verbatim).** Abstract: *"the error rates of responses generated from diverse prompts are significantly lower compared to those produced by stationary prompts"*; results: *"it yields relative gains of 10.8% in EM@100 for reasoning, 9.6% for mathematics, and 9.5% in Pass@100 for code generation"*; and *"we further show that under majority voting, diversity may vanish."* The body's main formal object (ar5iv render) is **Theorem 4.3: `N_inj^k ≤ N_reg^k / C_k, where C_k = O(k)`** — perturbed (diverse) inputs have failure rate lower by a factor increasing in k. **Diversity is measured by four pairwise-similarity metrics (TF-IDF, CodeBERT, Levenshtein, token-Jaccard); lower similarity ⇒ higher diversity ⇒ better Pass@k** (their Table 1).
**Fairness flag.** The headline @100 numbers and the majority-voting one-liner ARE in the v4 abstract (verbatim-confirmed). BUT: (a) the body's selection uses a **ground-truth reward model** — "*Since our focus is on diversity injection, we use the ground truth reward model in our experiments where possible*" — so "right" is enforced by an oracle, not a gold-free selector; (b) the ar5iv body-render (likely an earlier version) shows **Pass@10** examples (0.2050→0.3150) and **no majority-voting discussion at all**, i.e. the @100 numbers and the MV caveat are abstract-level / late-version additions. **No causal mechanism for *why* MV kills the gain is given** — the prior report's "different-wrong jams consensus, errors reinforce not cancel" was an unsupported paraphrase and must be dropped.
**2. FIT.** Directly anchors §5(⑸) (P-prompt arm, "diversity predicts selection gain") and the gain~diversity regression. The diversity metric they validate is **pairwise-distance** — the same metric our §7 critiques.
**3. ERROR/divergence — does it confirm or complicate D-oracle?** **It complicates the surface story but ultimately confirms our correction.** Their pairwise-distance diversity *does* predict Pass@k — but only because selection is via an **oracle reward model**, which is exactly D-oracle made operational (the oracle can always pick the right-and-different one). The moment they switch to **majority voting (consensus, gold-free-ish), "diversity may vanish."** That is our unified-pool result in one sentence: pairwise-distance diversity helps *iff* you can verify; with a consensus selector it can null out. So our claim is **stronger and more specific than theirs** (we name the D-oracle≈0 / consensus-jam mechanism and measure recall collapse 61%→40%), and is not contradicted. **Prior-report over-claim:** citing this paper for a *mechanistic* consensus-jam explanation is unsupported; cite it only for (i) pairwise-diversity→oracle-BoN gain and (ii) the bare "MV may vanish" caveat.
**RW sentence.** Wang et al. (2502.11027) show prompt-diversity lowers best-of-N error under an oracle reward model yet "may vanish" under majority voting — empirically demarcating exactly the verifier-vs-consensus boundary at which our pairwise-distance pool ceased to help.

### 1.2 — VB/VF separation theorem: 2502.12118 (v2, 2025-02-18)
**"Scaling Test-Time Compute Without Verification or RL is Suboptimal"** — Amrith Setlur, Nived Rajaraman, Sergey Levine, Aviral Kumar.

**1. Claim/theorem (verbatim, ar5iv).** **Theorem 5.8**: the VB and VF policies satisfy *"Jr(π^nvb) − Jr(π^nvf) = Ω̃(H/√n)"* when the base is heterogeneous with *"σ~b = Ω(H)"* and *"c0-anticoncentrated."* **Heterogeneity (Property 5.2):** *"σ²π,x := Σ_{h=1}^H E[Var_{a~π}[Q^πe(s_h,a_h)] | x]"* (variance over token sequences achieving correct answers). **Anti-concentration (Property 5.6):** *"base LLM πb is c-anticoncentrated if min_x c_x(κ_x) ≥ c, where c_x(ε) := Pr[r(τ) ≥ E[r(τ)] + σ_{b,x}√ε]"* (base samples above-mean-reward traces with constant probability). Abstract condition: heterogeneity is over *"correct solution traces (e.g., different lengths, styles, etc.)."*
**Fairness flag.** **The formal object is a fine-tuning separation, NOT test-time selection of a fixed pool** — *"finetuning πb to make efficient use of test-time compute."* Applying it to our gold-free pool selection is an **extension/analogy**, which our FIELD_GAP §5.5 already flags. Honest to keep that flag.
**2. FIT.** Theoretical anchor for §0 Δhetero +13.6 and the "selection gain = diversity function" law. The gap Ω̃(H/√n) scales with heterogeneity H — "no diversity ⇒ no verification advantage."
**3. ERROR/divergence — confirm D-oracle?** **Strongly confirms, and is the cleanest theory match.** The heterogeneity that earns the Ω̃(H/√n) advantage is variance **over *correct* traces** (Q^πe is the value under the correct-answer policy) and anti-concentration is about **above-mean *reward*** mass — i.e. the diversity that counts is diversity *among good answers*, not raw output spread. That is **D-oracle/D-unique by another name**: different-AND-right. P-lora's pairwise-distance diversity does NOT satisfy this — its extra spread was over wrong traces (Δoracle≈0), so by Theorem 5.8's own definition it contributes ~0 to H. **No over-claim in the prior report here** — but tighten the citation to say the theorem's "heterogeneity" is over *correct/high-reward* traces (this is the part that makes it support D-oracle specifically, not pairwise distance).
**RW sentence.** Setlur et al.'s Ω̃(H/√n) verifier advantage is keyed to heterogeneity *over correct/high-reward traces* and anti-concentration of reward — a fine-tuning-time formalization of our D-oracle claim that diversity must be among right answers to be selectable.

### 1.3 — DivPO: 2501.18101 (v4, 2025-05-22)
**"Diverse Preference Optimization"** — Lanchantin, A. Chen, Dhuliawala, P. Yu, Weston, Sukhbaatar, Kulikov.

**1. Claim/rule (verbatim).** *"Post-training of language models ... tends to sharpen the output probability distribution and reduce the diversity of generated responses."* Pair rule: *"selecting chosen examples as being more rare but high quality, while rejected examples are more common, but low quality."* Gains vs DPO: persona +45.6%, story +74.6%, general IF +46.2% with +2.4% winrate.
**Fairness flag.** Gains are on **persona/story/creative IF** (open-ended generation). No tool-DAG / narrow-answer task. Abstract states no limitation. Numbers are relative diversity %, paired with a small winrate gain — defensible but domain-specific.
**2. FIT.** §5(⑸) v4 prereg ("DivPO-style pair selection / GEM in v4 re-mining cycle"). Our concrete adaptation: add a "chosen's in-pool frequency penalty" rule to the miner.
**3. ERROR/divergence — confirm D-oracle?** **Confirms, mechanically.** DivPO's chosen criterion is **rare AND high-quality** — it explicitly *gates rarity by quality*. That is the training-time analogue of "different-AND-right": it will not promote a rare-but-wrong response (which is exactly P-lora's failure mode). So DivPO is *aligned with* the D-oracle correction, not in tension. **Caveat for us:** DivPO maximizes diversity *of the policy's own outputs*; we need diversity *across pool members that still each hit correct answers* — DivPO would help each adapter stay un-sharpened but does not by itself guarantee cross-member D-oracle>0. No prior-report over-claim.
**RW sentence.** DivPO's "rare *but high-quality*" chosen rule is the preference-learning embodiment of different-AND-right, directly motivating a frequency-penalty term in our v4 miner — though it controls within-policy spread, not cross-member oracle coverage.

### 1.4 — GEM: 2408.16673 (v2, 2025-04-05, ICLR 2025)
**"Preserving Diversity in Supervised Fine-Tuning of Large Language Models"** — Z. Li, C. Chen, T. Xu, Z. Qin, J. Xiao, Z.-Q. Luo, R. Sun.

**1. Claim/numbers (verbatim + body tables).** *"connects to the problem of reverse KL minimization with entropy regularization"*; *"GEM achieves comparable downstream performance to CE while significantly enhancing output diversity"*; *"This increased diversity translates to performance gains in test-time compute scaling for chat and code generation tasks."* **Body tables (ar5iv):** HumanEval Pass@100 **70.88% (CE) → 78.48% (GEM-LS) = +7.6 pts (10.7% rel)**; AlpacaEval BON@32 46.61%→49.46% (~+3 pts); GSM8K BON@32 +2.5 pts / MV +4.8 pts. Diversity measured by **n-gram (intra), Self-BLEU (inter), Sentence-BERT cosine** — higher = more diverse.
**Fairness flag.** The "+7.6 / up to 7 points" the prior report flagged as Ⓢ-only **is REAL and now verified from the body** — HumanEval Pass@100, general UltraFeedback fine-tuning. Win is on standard benchmarks, not custom. **Resolve the prior report's flag: "GEM up to 7 pts" is verified, cite HumanEval Pass@100.**
**2. FIT.** §5(⑸) v4 prereg, GEM-style SFT loss. **Most directly relevant of all alignment-collapse papers** because its win is *test-time-scaling BoN*, our exact metric.
**3. ERROR/divergence — confirm D-oracle?** **Confirms — and is the best evidence for "worth the retrain."** GEM is *the* demonstration that a diversity-preserving SFT objective **cashes out as BoN/Pass@100 gains** (not just intrinsic diversity). That implies its preserved diversity is the selectable, D-oracle-positive kind (otherwise Pass@100 would not move). **Complication:** GEM uses Self-BLEU/embedding (pairwise-distance-style) for its *diversity* metric, yet its *win* is on Pass@k — so GEM does NOT itself separate pairwise-distance from D-oracle; it only shows that *for their tasks* the two move together. Our autopsy is the case where they came apart. So GEM supports "retrain is worth it" but does NOT by itself validate the D-oracle metric over pairwise-distance.
**RW sentence.** GEM is the canonical proof that entropy-regularized SFT diversity converts to test-time Pass@100 gains (+7.6 on HumanEval), making it the strongest case for a v4 retrain — though it never separates pairwise-distance from oracle-contributing diversity, which our autopsy does.

### 1.5 — Price of Format: 2505.18949 (v1, 2025-05-25)
**"The Price of Format: Diversity Collapse in LLMs"** — Yun, An, Wang, Peng, Shang.

**1. Claim (verbatim).** *"output diversity is primarily governed by the presence or absence of structural tokens, with minimal formatting yielding the most diverse outputs"*; *"diversity collapse persists even under high-temperature sampling."* Format helps structure-sensitive tasks (GSM8K, IFEval), marginal on knowledge tasks (MMLU).
**Fairness flag.** Abstract gives no quantified diversity-reduction table; the diversity *metric* is not specified in the abstract, and it does **not** distinguish correct-mode from raw diversity. So it speaks only to *total* diversity collapse, not to whether the lost diversity was useful.
**2. FIT.** Anchors the P-unguided arm (§5(⑸): "guided JSON is diversity self-harm"). Our TB §8.9f confirmed P-unguided > P-temp.
**3. ERROR/divergence — confirm D-oracle?** **Neutral-to-supportive, but be careful.** It supports "structure tokens compress diversity," which justifies trying unguided pools. But it does **not** tell us whether the compressed diversity was D-oracle-positive — so it cannot, on its own, justify the unguided arm as a *selection-gain* lever. Our own data already showed P-unguided raised pairwise diversity but the *unified* pool still failed on recall, so "remove format → more diversity" is necessary-not-sufficient. **No over-claim, but the prior report's framing should not imply format-removal = selection gain; it is diversity gain only.**
**RW sentence.** Yun et al. show structural tokens — not temperature — govern diversity collapse, justifying our P-unguided arm, but their undifferentiated diversity metric cannot tell us whether the recovered diversity is the selectable kind.

### 1.6 — min-p original 2407.01082 (v8, 2025-11-20, ICLR 2025 Oral) + dispute 2506.13681 (v2, 2025-06-19)
**Original** — Nguyen, Baker, Neo, Roush, Kirsch, Shwartz-Ziv: *"min-p sampling improves both the quality and diversity of generated text ... especially at higher temperatures"*; mechanism = *"dynamic truncation ... using the top token's probability as a scaling factor."* Benchmarks GPQA/GSM8K/AlpacaEval Creative Writing, Mistral+Llama 1B–123B, human eval preference.

**Fairness flag.** This is a **live dispute**; the rebuttal alleges unfair/over-stated wins (omitted human-eval data, unsubstantiated adoption counts). The rebuttal itself ran a fresh human eval that also failed to support min-p. **Both should be cited as contested; do not cite min-p as a validated diversity lever.**
**2. FIT.** §RQ1 decoding-time levers (temperature/truncation arm of P-temp).
**3. ERROR/divergence — confirm D-oracle?** **Tangential but supportive of caution.** Even the *pro* claim is about *quality+diversity together* (i.e. quality-gated), and the dispute argues there's no real gain once controlled. Neither side argues raw pairwise diversity alone helps selection. Consistent with treating min-p as, at most, a "high-temp stabilizer," never a primary D-oracle lever — exactly the prior report's stance. No over-claim.
**RW sentence.** The min-p claim of joint quality+diversity gains is sharply disputed by Schaeffer et al. on hyperparameter-control grounds, so it remains at most a high-temperature stabilizer and never a standalone diversity lever for our pool.

## 6. Final answers to the three closing questions

**(a) Does the literature support the D-oracle-not-pairwise-distance correction?**
**Yes — by assumption/construction, not by an explicit named theorem.** The support is convergent and strong:
- 2502.12118 Theorem 5.8 keys the verifier advantage to heterogeneity **over correct/high-reward traces** + reward anti-concentration — D-oracle in formal dress (fine-tuning regime; test-time is our extension).
- 2502.11027 shows pairwise-distance diversity helps **only under an oracle reward model** and **"may vanish" under majority voting** — empirically bracketing the verifier/consensus boundary where our pairwise pool failed.
- 2407.15343 attributes MBR gains to a *"more diverse **and higher quality**"* space (verbatim conjunction).
